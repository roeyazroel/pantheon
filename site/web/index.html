The heart of the game

Gods that think for themselves

You are Ilyra, a minor spirit on an island of villages. The villagers live their own lives, and all you have is what they believe about you. Three to seven rival gods want the same people, and they aren't scripted opponents. Each one is an agent.

Always on

Instincts

A built-in AI that never sleeps. Every few seconds a god weighs every miracle it could cast on every village, sets itself goals (protect its home, convert a village, weaken a rival, prepare for drought or plague) and weighs every alliance, war, treaty and council vote by its personality and temper.

It needs no network, and with it alone the game is complete.

A mind

A language model wakes when something matters to the god, reads the world from the god's side, and decides what it says and does. Its decisions steer the instincts, which carry its plans out between its turns.

Claude, GPT, Gemini, or a model on your own computer through Ollama.

Meet your pantheon

Every new world's rival gods are born with it: a name and an epithet, a history the island tells of it, a voice, a desire and a dread, a personality, and a temper that tilts how it plays. Type any seed: these are exactly the gods you'll meet when you start a New World with it and the offline scribes write them. With a model chosen, the model writes them instead.

Diplomacy in your own words

Talk to them

Press Speak on a god's card and the world pauses while you talk. Type anything, or pick one of three replies the god's mind suggests. A conversation can make an alliance, a peace or any of eleven treaties, and a god can offer you an alliance, ask for peace, declare war or break your alliance, right there in the conversation.

Talking with Wynlius the Iron Hand. Without a model you put deals to it; with one, you type freely.

A god makes its own proposals mid-conversation.

Words tip the balance; they don't buy it

A god acts only on its own judgement: its trust, fear, grudges and ambitions. What you say builds or burns a little goodwill, which can lean that judgement by at most 0.15, and which halves every 4 minutes. Try it with the game's own numbers:

Your conscience and your guide

An angel that becomes what you do

An angel hovers at your hand. It reads the world's memory of your deeds, never a score, and changes to match: radiant, kind, unformed, torn, stern or fallen. It comes when something happens, speaks in the voice you've given it, and suggests what to do next. Set a god's deeds and see its angel, computed by the game's own rules.

A giant that learns from you

Creatures with a voice

Your Creature lives, works and learns on its own. Reward it and it does more of what it was doing; punish it and it does less. It grows to level ten and walks the paths its deeds call it to: healer, guardian, missionary, warrior, destroyer, trickster. Every Creature has a mind too: it speaks out loud and chooses what to do next, but never against your orders.

Pillars: villages, in the colour of the god they follow. White stones: holy sites.

☼Seasons and weather

A ten-minute year. Clouds blow in from the sea; dry spells parch the fields; snow lies on the hills and melts into the rivers in spring; hurricanes tear roofs off far inland.

≈Rivers and floods

Rivers run from the highlands to the sea and lakes fill the hollows. Heavy rain bursts their banks: houses fall, people drown, and the silt enriches the fields.

▲Disasters

Wildfires run with the wind. Earthquakes bring houses down and raise tsunamis. The volcano wakes. Tornadoes, falling stars, blight and locusts.

✦Great events

The plague spreads along roads. An eclipse terrifies every believer. A reformation sweeps your faith. The ancient beast wakes and walks from village to village.

⌂Villages of their own

They farm, build temples and walls, learn crafts, hold doctrines like pacifism or militarism, send out settlers, migrate, and may turn their backs on every god.

⚖Diplomacy with teeth

Eleven kinds of treaty, some signed in secret. Favours owed. The Divine Council's votes and decrees. Wars for conquest, tribute or a holy site, and betrayals remembered longest.

Fair by design

A model plays; it doesn't referee. Every act a mind takes goes through the same checks as your commands, and answers are read strictly. A god written badly is rewritten by the scribes.

The world never waits

A model's answer is taken between the simulation's steps. While a mind thinks, its instincts carry on, so nothing in play waits on the network.

Your key stays yours

Each key is kept on your computer, apart from your other settings, and sent only to its own provider. Never logged, never saved in a world, never sent to other players.

Questions

Do I need an API key to play?

No. The game is complete without one: the built-in AI plays every god, and the offline scribes write and speak for them. A key (or a model on your own computer) gives the gods minds of their own.

What does a model cost me?

Questions are billed to your own account with the provider: one for each world made, one for each thing you say to a god, and the minds' own turns (a god's is about 1,500 tokens asked and a few hundred answered; a Creature's about 600). An hourly cap (300 questions by default) bounds it, and quick models take the frequent turns. A local model costs nothing.

Can I talk a god into giving me the island?

No. Talk only leans a god's own judgement, and only by a little; every act goes through the game's rules. A god nearly ready to ally can be won over, and one nearly ready to strike can be provoked, but no prompt makes a god that distrusts you an ally.

Does the game need the internet?

Only if you choose an online model. Everything else runs on your computer, and every failure falls back to the offline scribes.

Is the site's "Meet your pantheon" the real thing?

Yes. It's the game's scribe code, ported to JavaScript and tested against the game's C# for dozens of seeds and every world size, word for word. The angel and the talk demonstrations use the game's own rules and numbers, and the islands come from the game's world generator.
